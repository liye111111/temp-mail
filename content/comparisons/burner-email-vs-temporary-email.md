**Burner email** is a broad, informal term for an address intended for limited use. **Temporary email** usually means an automatically generated inbox that expires after a short period. A temporary address can be a burner address, but a manually created secondary account can also be described as a burner. For background, see the overviews of [disposable email addresses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disposable_email_address) and [email aliases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Email_alias).

## Quick comparison

| Option | Typical lifespan | Receives mail | Sends mail | Recovery | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Temporary inbox | Minutes or hours | Yes | Usually no | Usually none | One-time, low-risk messages |
| Burner account | User-defined | Usually | Often | Sometimes | A separate identity for limited use |
| Email alias | Until disabled | Forwards mail | Provider-dependent | Through primary account | Ongoing address separation |
| Permanent email | Long-term | Yes | Yes | Yes | Important accounts and relationships |

## Choose a temporary inbox when

Use temporary email when you only need an incoming message for a brief, low-risk task and will not need the address later. GetOpenInbox provides a 10-minute receiving session without registration and displays common authentication and link-warning signals.

## Choose an alias or secondary account when

Use an alias when you want to identify which service shared an address, disable one address later, or keep ongoing mail separated. Use a secondary permanent account when you need to send messages, retain receipts, reset passwords, or maintain access.

## The deciding question

Ask: **What happens if I lose this address and every message in it?** If the answer involves money, identity, confidential information, or an account you value, do not use temporary email. If nothing important is lost, a [temporary inbox](/) may be the simpler choice.
